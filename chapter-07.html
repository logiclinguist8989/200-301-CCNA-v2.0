Chapter 7Edge-host ports: VLAN, PoE, port channel and LACP

🖼️ 4 figures⏱️ ~23 min read📝 Self-check: 14 questions

Objective covered: 2.2 Configure Layer 2 switch port attributes for edge-host connectivity (VLAN, PoE, port channel, and LACP) for 2.2.a desktops, printers and IoT appliances, 2.2.b wireless access points (standalone and controller-based), 2.2.c VoIP phones, 2.2.d virtualized hosts and 2.2.e network appliances.

This objective asks one practical question: a device is plugged into this port — what should the port's configuration be? The answer depends on how many VLANs the device needs, whether it needs power, and whether it uses more than one cable.

7.1 Learning objectives

After this chapter you can:

  1. Apply a secure baseline to every edge port.
  2. Choose the right port profile for each of the five device types in the objective (Figure 7.1).
  3. Configure and verify PoE, including power budgets and inline power states.
  4. Configure a data VLAN plus a voice VLAN for an IP phone with a PC behind it.
  5. Configure switch ports for standalone and controller-based access points.
  6. Configure trunk and LACP edge ports for virtualized hosts and network appliances.
  7. Troubleshoot edge-port problems from show-command output.

Lab itThis chapter is hands-on. Once the theory clicks, jump to Packet Tracer Lab 7.1 — A PoE access switch for every edge device (Intermediate) and build it yourself — reading about a network is good, breaking and fixing one is better.

7.2 The edge-port baseline

An edge port connects to an end device rather than another switch. Every edge port in this guide starts from the same baseline; the rest of the chapter only adds to it.

SW4
SW4(config)# interface gigabitethernet1/0/1
SW4(config-if)# description PC-A Staff
SW4(config-if)# switchport mode access              ! no DTP, never becomes a trunk
SW4(config-if)# switchport access vlan 10
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree portfast             ! forward immediately (Chapter 9)
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree bpduguard enable     ! shut the port if a switch appears (Chapter 9)
SW4(config-if)# no shutdown
Baseline item Why Covered in depth
description Documentation; checked against CDP/LLDP Chapter 8
switchport mode access Stops DTP from forming an accidental trunk Chapter 5
switchport access vlan Places the device in the right subnet Chapter 5
spanning-tree portfast Hosts get a link in about a second instead of \~30 s, so DHCP does not time out Chapter 9
spanning-tree bpduguard enable Protects against someone plugging in a switch Chapter 9
Port security, DHCP snooping, storm control Limit MACs, stop rogue DHCP, contain floods Chapter 23

7.3 Desktops, printers and IoT appliances (2.2.a)

These devices need one VLAN and an untagged access port: the baseline from 7.2 with the right VLAN.

SW4
SW4(config)# interface gigabitethernet1/0/3
SW4(config-if)# description Printer1 - 2nd floor
SW4(config-if)# switchport mode access
SW4(config-if)# switchport access vlan 40
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree portfast
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree bpduguard enable
SW4(config-if)# power inline never                ! printer is mains-powered; no PoE

Design points:

  • Printers usually keep a fixed address (static, or a DHCP reservation) because users and print servers point at it.
  • IoT appliances (cameras, badge readers, sensors, smart TVs) go in their own VLAN. They are often poorly secured, so ACLs (Chapter 20) should limit what they can reach. Many are PoE-powered.
  • Speed and duplex stay on auto for all three (Chapter 1).
  • Configure groups of identical ports once with interface range, for example interface range gigabitethernet1/0/5 - 20.

7.4 Power over Ethernet (PoE)

PoE delivers DC power over the same twisted-pair cable as data, so phones, APs, cameras and IoT sensors need no power socket. The switch is the PSE (power sourcing equipment); the device is the PD (powered device).

How a PoE port powers a device:

  1. Detection — the switch applies a low test voltage and looks for a PD's signature resistance. A PC NIC has none, so it never receives power.
  2. Classification — the PD signals a power class (and with LLDP-MED or CDP can negotiate a more exact amount).
  3. Power-up — if the switch's remaining power budget covers the request, it supplies power; otherwise it denies power and logs it.
  4. Monitoring — if the PD is unplugged or draws too much, power is removed.
Standard Common name Power at the switch port Typical devices
IEEE 802.3af (Type 1) PoE 15.4 W IP phones, basic cameras
IEEE 802.3at (Type 2) PoE+ 30 W Most Wi-Fi 5/6 APs, PTZ cameras
IEEE 802.3bt Type 3 PoE++ / 4-pair 60 W High-end APs, video phones
IEEE 802.3bt Type 4 PoE++ 90 W Displays, small switches

The device receives slightly less than the switch sends (cable loss). Cisco's UPOE and UPOE+ are proprietary equivalents at 60 W and 90 W.

Configuration (interface mode; most PoE switches default to auto):

Command Effect
power inline auto Detect and power any PD up to the port's maximum (default)
power inline auto max 15400 Same, but cap the port at 15.4 W (value in milliwatts)
power inline static max 30000 Reserve 30 W for this port in advance, even before a device connects
power inline never Disable PoE on the port

Read it as: the switch has 390 W of PoE budget, 21.7 W is in use; the phone on Gi1/0/2 and the AP on Gi1/0/4 are powered; Gi1/0/1 has a PC (no PD detected, so off); Gi1/0/3 was set to never (Admin off).

PoE troubleshooting: a device with no lights on a PoE port means check (1) the port is not never, (2) the switch's remaining budget, (3) the device's class against the port's maximum (an AP needing PoE+ on a PoE-only port may boot with reduced radios or not at all), (4) the cable. A state of faulty or err-disable usually means overcurrent or a bad cable or device.

Memory trickPoE standards go alphabetically up in power: af (PoE, 15.4 W) → at (PoE+, 30 W) → bt (PoE++, 60 W and up).

7.5 IP phones and the voice VLAN (2.2.c)

Offices save cabling by plugging the PC into the back of the IP phone, so one switch port serves two devices in two VLANs: the phone in the voice VLAN and the PC in the data VLAN. Separating voice lets you give it priority (QoS) and keep it away from user traffic.

How it works (Figure 7.2):

In Figure 7.2 the dashed arrow is the CDP or LLDP-MED message that tells the phone its voice VLAN. The highlighted link between switch and phone carries both kinds of frame shown in the cards beneath it, and it also carries PoE power. Only untagged data crosses the phone-to-PC link.

  1. The switch powers the phone with PoE.
  2. The switch tells the phone its voice VLAN using CDP (Cisco phones) or LLDP-MED (other vendors): "use VLAN 20 for voice".
  3. The phone contains a small three-port switch: one port to the wall, one for the PC, one internal for the phone itself.
  4. The phone sends its own traffic tagged with VLAN 20 and CoS 5 (the 802.1Q PCP field from Figure 5.2).
  5. The PC's frames pass through the phone untagged, and the switch places them in the access VLAN 10.
  6. The phone gets its IP address by DHCP in the voice subnet (10.10.20.0/24 in this guide).

Configuration:

SW4
SW4(config)# vlan 20
SW4(config-vlan)# name VOICE
SW4(config-vlan)# exit
SW4(config)# interface gigabitethernet1/0/2
SW4(config-if)# description IP phone + PC-B
SW4(config-if)# switchport mode access
SW4(config-if)# switchport access vlan 10            ! data VLAN for the PC
SW4(config-if)# switchport voice vlan 20             ! voice VLAN for the phone
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree portfast
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree bpduguard enable
SW4(config-if)# power inline auto

Verification:

Exam points:

  • The port is still an access port (static access); it is not a trunk and does not appear in show interfaces trunk, even though voice frames are tagged.
  • The voice VLAN must exist on the switch and be carried on the uplink trunks (allowed vlan add 20).
  • Older Cisco documentation called this an "auxiliary VLAN".
  • QoS trust settings for voice (for example mls qos trust device cisco-phone on some platforms) are beyond the v2.0 objectives.

Troubleshooting phones: phone dark → PoE (7.4); phone gets an address in the data subnet → voice VLAN missing on the port, or CDP/LLDP disabled so the phone never learned VLAN 20; phone works but PC does not → access VLAN wrong; both fail beyond the switch → VLAN 20 or 10 not allowed on the uplink trunk.

7.6 Wireless access points (2.2.b)

An AP bridges Wi-Fi clients onto the wired network. Each SSID (wireless network name) maps to a VLAN — for example Staff-WiFi to VLAN 30 and Guest to VLAN 35. Where that mapping happens decides how the switch port is configured (Figure 7.3).

On the left of Figure 7.3 the AP itself maps each SSID to a VLAN, so its port must carry all of them as a trunk. On the right the dashed CAPWAP tunnel carries client traffic from the AP across the switched network to the WLC, which maps SSIDs to VLANs on its own trunk; the AP's port needs only VLAN 99.

Standalone (autonomous) AP. The AP is configured individually and bridges each SSID straight onto its VLAN. Its switch port must therefore be a trunk: the AP's own management address is usually in the native VLAN (untagged), and each SSID's VLAN is tagged.

SW4
SW4(config)# interface gigabitethernet1/0/4
SW4(config-if)# description AP-Lobby (standalone)
SW4(config-if)# switchport mode trunk
SW4(config-if)# switchport trunk native vlan 99          ! AP management, untagged
SW4(config-if)# switchport trunk allowed vlan 30,35,99
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree portfast trunk
SW4(config-if)# power inline auto

Controller-based (lightweight) AP in local mode. The AP is managed by a wireless LAN controller (WLC). It builds a CAPWAP tunnel to the WLC — control on UDP 5246 (encrypted with DTLS) and data on UDP 5247 — and sends every client frame inside that tunnel. The WLC places client traffic on the right VLAN. The AP's switch port therefore needs only the AP management VLAN, as an access port:

SW4
SW4(config)# interface gigabitethernet1/0/5
SW4(config-if)# description LAP-1 (controller-based, local mode)
SW4(config-if)# switchport mode access
SW4(config-if)# switchport access vlan 99
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree portfast
SW4(config-if)# power inline auto

The WLC's own uplink is a trunk carrying the client VLANs (30, 35) and its management VLAN, often bundled for redundancy. Older AireOS controllers bundle with static mode on (no LACP), while Catalyst 9800 controllers support LACP — check the platform before choosing the channel mode.

AP type Switch port Why
Standalone (autonomous) Trunk; native = AP management; SSID VLANs tagged The AP itself puts each SSID on its VLAN
Controller-based, local mode Access in the AP management VLAN Client traffic is tunnelled to the WLC
Controller-based, FlexConnect (local switching) Trunk, like standalone The AP switches client traffic locally at a branch
Cloud-managed (for example Meraki) Usually trunk Managed from the cloud; traffic is bridged locally

How a lightweight AP joins a WLC (step by step): the AP boots on PoE → gets an IP address by DHCP in VLAN 99 → discovers the WLC (DHCP option 43, DNS name CISCO-CAPWAP-CONTROLLER, or broadcast) → builds the CAPWAP tunnel → downloads its configuration → starts its SSIDs.

Troubleshooting APs: AP has no power or reduced radios → PoE class or budget (7.4); standalone AP clients get no address in one SSID → that SSID's VLAN missing from the trunk; lightweight AP never joins → no DHCP or route to the WLC, or UDP 5246/5247 blocked; lightweight AP on a trunk with the wrong native VLAN → it gets an address in the wrong subnet.

7.7 Virtualized hosts (2.2.d)

A hypervisor host carries VMs in several VLANs over its uplinks (Chapter 4, Figure 4.2), so its ports are trunks, usually two or more for redundancy. Whether they form an LACP bundle depends on the host's NIC-teaming mode: bundle only when the host uses LACP.

Two-uplink host using LACP:

SW4
SW4(config)# interface range gigabitethernet1/0/11 - 12
SW4(config-if-range)# description ESXi-01 vmnic0-1
SW4(config-if-range)# switchport mode trunk
SW4(config-if-range)# switchport trunk native vlan 999
SW4(config-if-range)# switchport trunk allowed vlan 10,30,99
SW4(config-if-range)# switchport nonegotiate
SW4(config-if-range)# channel-group 11 mode active
SW4(config-if-range)# exit
SW4(config)# interface port-channel 11
SW4(config-if)# switchport mode trunk
SW4(config-if)# switchport trunk native vlan 999
SW4(config-if)# switchport trunk allowed vlan 10,30,99
SW4(config-if)# spanning-tree portfast trunk           ! host does not bridge between its NICs

Host using independent teaming (no LACP): configure both ports as identical trunks without channel-group, each with spanning-tree portfast trunk.

Newer IOS releases write the PortFast command as spanning-tree portfast edge trunk. Use PortFast on host trunks only when the host does not run STP or bridge between its NICs, which is normal for hypervisors.

7.8 Network appliances (2.2.e)

Network appliances are dedicated devices that provide a service: firewalls, wireless LAN controllers, load balancers, intrusion prevention systems, WAN optimizers. They commonly connect in one of three ways:

Connection Switch port Example
One interface per network Access ports in different VLANs Small firewall: inside port in VLAN 10, DMZ port in VLAN 50
Subinterfaces on one link Trunk Firewall "router-on-a-stick" for several inside VLANs
Bundled for capacity and redundancy Trunk inside an LACP port-channel Data-centre firewall or WLC with two or four uplinks
SW4
SW4(config)# interface range gigabitethernet1/0/21 - 22
SW4(config-if-range)# description FW1 inside, Po21 members
SW4(config-if-range)# switchport mode trunk
SW4(config-if-range)# switchport trunk allowed vlan 10,30,40,99
SW4(config-if-range)# channel-group 21 mode active

Design notes: appliances are often deployed in high-availability pairs; each unit connects to the switches the same way, and the pair shares a virtual IP. Appliances in transparent (bridging) mode can create Layer 2 loops, so do not enable PortFast or BPDU filtering on them without following the vendor's design.

7.10 Troubleshooting edge ports

Symptom Likely cause Fix
IP phone dark PoE disabled (never), budget exhausted, or class too high for the port power inline auto; check show power inline
Phone gets a data-VLAN address No switchport voice vlan, or CDP/LLDP disabled Add the voice VLAN; enable CDP or LLDP
PC behind phone has no network Wrong access VLAN, or data VLAN not on the uplink trunk Correct switchport access vlan; allowed vlan add
Standalone AP: one SSID's clients get no address That VLAN missing from the AP trunk Add it to the allowed list
Lightweight AP never joins the WLC AP VLAN has no DHCP or no route to the WLC; CAPWAP blocked Fix DHCP/routing; permit UDP 5246–5247
AP boots with reduced radios PoE class higher than the port supplies Use a PoE+ (or higher) port; raise the port maximum
Host VMs flap or lose connectivity Teaming mode and switch bundling disagree Match LACP on both sides, or remove channel-group
Port err-disabled after connecting a device BPDU guard saw a switch (or an appliance in bridge mode) Remove the switch or redesign; bounce the port (Chapter 9)
Printer unreachable from users Printer in its own VLAN but no inter-VLAN routing or an ACL blocks it Check gateway SVI and ACLs

7.12 Chapter 7 summary notes (exam revision)

  • Every edge port: description, switchport mode access (or trunk where needed), correct VLAN, spanning-tree portfast, spanning-tree bpduguard enable, unused ports shut.
  • Desktops, printers, IoT (2.2.a): access ports; IoT in its own VLAN; printers static or reserved.
  • PoE: PSE (switch) powers PD; detection → classification → power if budget allows. 802.3af 15.4 W, 802.3at (PoE+) 30 W, 802.3bt 60/90 W. power inline auto | never | static; verify with show power inline.
  • IP phone (2.2.c): switchport access vlan 10 + switchport voice vlan 20; phone learns VLAN via CDP/LLDP-MED; voice tagged (CoS 5), PC untagged; port stays access and is not in show interfaces trunk.
  • APs (2.2.b): standalone → trunk (native = AP management, SSID VLANs tagged); controller-based local mode → access in the AP VLAN, CAPWAP (UDP 5246/5247) to the WLC; FlexConnect local switching → trunk. WLC uplink = trunk.
  • Virtualized hosts (2.2.d): trunk with all VM VLANs; LACP bundle only if the host's teaming uses LACP; spanning-tree portfast trunk.
  • Appliances (2.2.e): access per interface, a trunk, or a trunk inside an LACP bundle; often in HA pairs.

Common conceptual mistakes

Mistake Correct idea
"A port with a voice VLAN is a trunk" It is an access port with a voice VLAN
"Every AP needs a trunk" Controller-based local-mode APs use an access port
"PoE will power any device" Only detected PDs, and only within the switch's budget and the port's maximum
"Always bundle a server's two NICs" Bundle only when the host's teaming uses LACP

Gi1/0/7 connects to an IP phone with a PC behind it. Data is VLAN 10 and voice is VLAN 20. Which two interface commands are essential besides switchport mode access?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

switchport access vlan 10 and switchport voice vlan 20. (PortFast, BPDU guard and PoE auto are also normal.)

Does Gi1/0/7 from question 1 appear in show interfaces trunk? Why or why not?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

No. The port is an access port with a voice VLAN (operational mode static access), so it is not listed as a trunk even though the phone's frames are tagged.

How do the phone's frames and the PC's frames differ on the link between the switch and the phone?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

The phone tags its voice frames with VLAN 20 and CoS 5; the PC's frames are untagged and placed in access VLAN 10.

Which standard is PoE+ and how much power can the switch port supply?

B. 802.3af is the original 15.4 W PoE; 802.3bt is the higher-power standard; 802.3ad is link aggregation (LACP), not PoE.

5Output interpretationthink, then reveal

show power inline shows Gi1/0/1 auto off 0.0 n/a. A PC is connected and working. Is there a fault?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

No. off with no device class means no powered device was detected — a PC does not request PoE. Admin auto means the port would power a PD if one were connected.

6Designthink, then reveal

A standalone AP broadcasts Staff-WiFi (VLAN 30) and Guest (VLAN 35); its management address is in VLAN 99. How should its switch port be configured?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

As a trunk: native VLAN 99 (AP management, untagged) and allowed VLANs 30, 35 and 99, with spanning-tree portfast trunk and PoE.

Did you get it?
7Designthink, then reveal

A lightweight AP in local mode is managed by a WLC. Which switch-port configuration is typical, and why?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

An access port in the AP management VLAN. Client traffic is carried inside the CAPWAP tunnel to the WLC, which maps SSIDs to VLANs, so the AP's port does not need the client VLANs.

Did you get it?

Which UDP ports does CAPWAP use?

C. 5246 is CAPWAP control, 5247 is data. 67/68 are DHCP, 161/162 SNMP, 4789 is VXLAN.

9Multiple response — choose twoauto-graded

Which protocols can tell an IP phone its voice VLAN?

Choose 2

A and C. CDP (Cisco phones) and LLDP-MED (multi-vendor) advertise the voice VLAN. DTP negotiates trunks; VTP shares VLAN lists; STP prevents loops.

10Troubleshootingthink, then reveal

A new IP phone receives an address in 10.10.10.0/24 (the data subnet) instead of 10.10.20.0/24. What is the most likely cause?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

The port has no switchport voice vlan 20, or CDP/LLDP is disabled, so the phone never learns its voice VLAN and requests an address untagged in the data VLAN.

11Troubleshootingthink, then reveal

A new Wi-Fi 6 AP boots but only one radio comes up. The port is on an older switch. What should you check first?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

PoE: the AP probably needs PoE+ (30 W) or more, but the port supplies only 802.3af (15.4 W) or the switch's budget is short. Check show power inline.

12Configurationthink, then reveal

A hypervisor host uses LACP teaming on two NICs connected to Gi1/0/11–12 and carries VLANs 10, 30 and 99. Outline the switch configuration.

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

interface range gi1/0/11 - 12 → switchport mode trunk, switchport trunk allowed vlan 10,30,99 (plus native VLAN) → channel-group 11 mode active; then on interface port-channel 11 repeat the trunk settings and add spanning-tree portfast trunk.

Did you get it?
13Designthink, then reveal

Where should a building's IP security cameras be placed, and what else should protect them?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

In a separate IoT/camera VLAN, usually PoE-powered, with ACLs (Chapter 20) allowing only the video recorder and management systems to reach them.

14Evaluate an AI assistantthink, then reveal

On a standalone AP's trunk port, Guest clients (VLAN 35) get no address while Staff clients work. A network assistant recommends: "Change the AP port to switchport access vlan 35." Is this a good fix?

Answer it in your head (or on paper) first.

No. An access port in VLAN 35 would carry only Guest traffic and break Staff-WiFi and the AP's management. The likely fault is VLAN 35 missing from the trunk's allowed list (or not created); fix it with switchport trunk allowed vlan add 35.

Did you get it?
